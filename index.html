Challenge

Open Science project

ADPKD patients face uncertainty regarding their future kidney function, impacting decisions and treatment plans. Current prediction tools lack precision and do not allow integration of different data types. This knowledge is crucial to guide therapeutic decision‑making.

Furthermore, including data across centres would be pivotal to improve such tools and allow for comparison of disease‑courses — non‑trivial, hindering the ability to create reliable predictive models.

Solution

ARTICYST will build an infrastructure to integrate clinical, genotype, MRI, and proteomic data using unbiased model building across multiple cohorts. A dedicated mass spectrometry pipeline, database design, and clinical data exchange will enable predictive power and future assay panels.

The framework will scale across centres and biosamples and will reach out to BBMRI‑ERIC and EATRIS for broader extension.

Scientific Impact

The platform can extend to similar challenges across non‑communicable diseases by establishing targeted diagnostic panels and collaborating with patient advocacy groups. Increasing the visibility of Open Science practices in clinical research will support future research and treatment advancements.

Results

  • German patient survey: gathered opinions toward Open Science in counselling and medicine and willingness to share medical data.
  • Patient outreach: short communication videos and articles published with PKD International channels.
  • Mapping of clinical database variables: framework for sharing and integrating data between two EU clinical sites.
